01
The problem

Headline yields are easy to quote. The assumptions behind them are not.

A listing gives a price and an asking rent. Whether that rent is achievable, what the property costs to hold and what could go wrong are left for the buyer to work out. Screening puts those questions first, before time and money are committed.

    Our Approach

    A disciplined way to test an opportunity.

    The method below is the proposed screening structure. Content marked for approval should be confirmed before launch.

    01

    Property screening

    Check the property and its context against the investor’s stated criteria.

    02

    Rental-demand assessment

    Consider who is likely to rent the property and how deep that demand is.

    03

    Income and yield analysis

    Translate achievable rent into gross and indicative net yield, with the workings shown.

    Comparable-property research

    Use comparable evidence to test price and rent assumptions.

    05

    Model costs, voids and downside cases rather than a single optimistic figure.

    06

    Risk identification

    List the legal, physical, regulatory and market risks that need checking.

    07

    Investment comparison

    Set one opportunity beside another on consistent assumptions.

    Property Intelligence

    The numbers, with their assumptions.

    Illustrative example · not a real property, client or recommendation

    A fictional £850,000 London flat shows how a screening presents income, costs and sensitivity. All figures are invented for illustration.

    Yields are before mortgage costs, tax, stamp duty and capital expenditure. Gross yield is annual rent divided by price. Indicative net yield is rent less the recurring costs listed, divided by price.

    Indicative net yield (%) as rent and total recurring costs vary. Highlighted cell is the base case.

    Risks and due diligence

    • Rent assumption is untested against current comparable evidence.
    • Service charge and ground rent could rise; lease terms unchecked.
    • Energy performance and regulatory requirements to be confirmed.
    • Void periods may exceed the four-week allowance.
    • Condition, capital expenditure and title still to be investigated.
